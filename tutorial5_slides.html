Financial Claims Scheme, Monetary Targeting, and Silicon Valley Bank.
Think & Discuss
“It is 2008. You have $100,000 in your bank. There are rumours that your bank may become insolvent, and the government does not guarantee your deposit. What would you do?”
Takeaway
You have a strong incentive to withdraw your money immediately.
Mechanism
If many depositors act on this incentive together, it generates a bank run, potentially collapsing the bank.
Think & Discuss
“If everyone is worried about banks, will it become easier or harder for banks to attract funding? What happens to the cost?”
Takeaway
Funding becomes harder to attract and more expensive as investors demand a higher risk premium.
Transmission Chain
Uncertainty → Withdrawals → Bank-run risk → Higher funding cost
Think & Discuss
“The government announces: deposits up to $250,000 are guaranteed. Does your decision change?”
Takeaway
Yes. Depositors stay put because the government compensates them if the bank fails.
Outcome
Eliminating bank-run risk substantially lowers banks' funding costs.
Policy Transmission
Deposit guarantee → Lower depositor risk → Fewer withdrawals → Lower bank-run risk → Lower funding cost
Think & Discuss
“Suppose banks now struggle to raise funds. I'm not a banker, so why should I care?”
Takeaway
Banks cannot intermediate effectively, so firms find it harder to finance productive investments.
Real Economy Impact
Lower investment directly drags down equilibrium output and employment.
Macroeconomic Transmission
Less bank funding → Less lending → Less investment → Lower output and employment
Theory: Quantity Equation
We start from the Quantity Theory identity: \( MV = PY \).
Think & Discuss
“What does the quantity theory tell us about long-run inflation?”
Takeaway
Long-run inflation equals money growth minus potential output growth: \( \pi = g_M - g_{Y^*} \).
Underlying Assumptions
Assumptions: Long-run output is independent of money, velocity is constant, and the central bank controls money supply.
Think & Discuss
“Suppose these assumptions are all true. Why might a central bank target money growth?”
Takeaway
Because controlling money growth (\( g_M \)) provides a direct mechanism to control long-run inflation (\( \pi \)).
Think & Discuss
“Potential output \( Y^* \). What's the practical problem?”
Takeaway
Potential output cannot be directly observed or measured, making the target money growth rate uncertain.
Think & Discuss
“The central bank controls base money \( M_0 \). Does that mean it controls broad money \( M = k M_0 \)?”
Takeaway
Only if \( k \) is constant, but the multiplier \( k \) fluctuates with commercial bank lending behavior.
Think & Discuss
“What about velocity? Can we safely assume \( V \) is constant?”
Takeaway
No. Velocity shifts dynamically with technological and financial payment innovations.

Think & Discuss: Stage 1
“SVB attracts lots of deposits from tech startups. Tell me what changes on the balance sheet (\( \uparrow \), \( \downarrow \) or unchanged)?”
Takeaway: Stage 1
Customer deposits \( \uparrow \) and initially Reserves \( \uparrow \) (both sides expand).
Think & Discuss: Stage 1
“Should SVB just leave all that incoming money as reserves?”
Takeaway: Stage 2
SVB purchases fixed-income securities (bonds) for higher return: \( \text{Reserves} \downarrow, \text{Bonds} \uparrow \).
Think & Discuss: Stage 3
“Interest rates now rise substantially. What happens to the value of SVB's existing fixed-income securities?”
Takeaway: Stage 3
\( i \uparrow \implies P_{\text{bond}} \downarrow \): The market value of SVB's existing securities plunges.
Think & Discuss: Stage 4
“Tech funding dries up and startups withdraw deposits (\( \text{Deposits} \downarrow \)). SVB has lots of assets — why is withdrawal a problem?”
Takeaway: Stage 4
Cash reserves are insufficient, forcing SVB to sell devalued securities at a loss to fund withdrawals.
Think & Discuss: Stage 5
“SVB announces that it has sold assets at a loss. You're another tech company with $10M deposited there. What do you do?”
Takeaway: Stage 5
Withdraw immediately.
Stage 5: Panic & Run
When everyone acts together, the news generates panic, producing a catastrophic bank run.
Complete SVB Failure Chain
Deposit inflows → Bond purchases → Interest rates rise → Bond values fall → Withdrawals → Forced sales → Realised losses → Panic & Bank run
