Question 1: Production Data
Minutes required to produce 1 unit of each product:
Person Beer Soda
Bill 30 min 15 min
Eric 60 min 30 min
Think & Discuss
“What is Bill’s opportunity cost for producing beer? Soda? What about Eric’s?”
Opportunity Cost Calculation
Bill: $1\text{ Beer} = \mathbf{2\text{ Sodas}}$  |  Eric: $1\text{ Beer} = \mathbf{2\text{ Sodas}}$.
In the 30 min Bill takes to brew 1 beer, he foregoes $30/15 = 2$ sodas.
In the 60 min Eric takes to brew 1 beer, he foregoes $60/30 = 2$ sodas.
Think & Discuss
“Are there possible gains from trade between Bill and Eric? Why or why not?”
Takeaway
No gains from trade exist because their opportunity costs are identical.
Trade requires specialisation, which only creates value when opportunity costs differ. Neither individual has a comparative advantage.
Core Principle
Nations gain most from trade when they have different relative productivities.
Differences in opportunity costs create a broader range of mutually advantageous terms of trade.
Question 2: Model Setup
Ricardian Economy ($L_1 = L_2 = 100$ workers)
Country Labour ($L$) Manufactures Productivity ($A_m$) Services Productivity ($A_s$)
Country 1 100 $A_m = 4$ $A_s = 8$
Country 2 100 $A_m^* = 3$ $A_s^* = 1$
Note: $A_i$ represents output per worker (productivity), not labor hours!
Deriving PPF Equations

Country 1 Resource Constraint

$\frac{Y_{m,1}}{4} + \frac{Y_{s,1}}{8} = 100$

$Y_{s,1} = 800 - 2 Y_{m,1}$

Intercepts: $(0, 800)$ & $(400, 0)$ | Slope: $-2$

Country 2 Resource Constraint

$\frac{Y_{m,2}}{3} + \frac{Y_{s,2}}{1} = 100$

$Y_{s,2} = 100 - \frac{1}{3} Y_{m,2}$

Intercepts: $(0, 100)$ & $(300, 0)$ | Slope: $-\frac{1}{3}$

PPF Geometry
Linear PPFs Reflect Constant Opportunity Costs
Country 1: Max $800S$ or $400M$ (Slope $= -2$)
Country 2: Max $100S$ or $300M$ (Slope $= -1/3$)
The absolute slope $|dY_s / dY_m| = A_s / A_m$ equals the opportunity cost of Manufactures.
Question 2(b): Absolute Advantage
Country 1 has an Absolute Advantage in BOTH goods.
Manufactures: $4 > 3$ Services: $8 > 1$
One worker produces more units of both manufactures and services in Country 1 than in Country 2.
Question 2(c): Comparative Advantage
Good Country 1 OC Country 2 OC Comparative Advantage
1 Manufacture ($M$) $\frac{8}{4} = \mathbf{2\text{ Services}}$ $\frac{1}{3} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{3}\text{ Services}}$ Country 2 (Lower OC)
1 Service ($S$) $\frac{4}{8} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}\text{ Manuf.}}$ $\frac{3}{1} = \mathbf{3\text{ Manuf.}}$ Country 1 (Lower OC)
Even with absolute disadvantage in both goods, Country 2 has a comparative advantage in Manufactures!
Question 2(d): Autarky Equilibrium
Consumers demand manufactures and services in equal amounts: $Y_m = Y_s = x$.
Preferences are Leontief (perfect complements), meaning neither good is consumed in excess of the other.
Country 1 Autarky Outcome
$x = 800 - 2x \implies x = \mathbf{266\frac{2}{3} \text{ units}}$
Labour in $M$: $\frac{266.67}{4} = \mathbf{66\frac{2}{3}}$ Labour in $S$: $\frac{266.67}{8} = \mathbf{33\frac{1}{3}}$
Twice as much labour is needed for manufactures because $A_m = 4$ is half of $A_s = 8$.
Country 2 Autarky Outcome
$x = 100 - \frac{1}{3}x \implies x = \mathbf{75 \text{ units}}$
Labour in $M$: $\frac{75}{3} = \mathbf{25}$ Labour in $S$: $\frac{75}{1} = \mathbf{75}$
Three times as much labour is needed for services because $A_s^* = 1$ is one-third of $A_m^* = 3$.
Challenge Presenters
“Country 1 is more productive at everything. Why would Country 1 EVER want to buy manufactures from Country 2?”
Expected Insight
Producing manufactures at home forces Country 1 to divert high-productivity service workers.
Importing $1M$ costs Country 1 only $1S$ on world markets, compared to giving up $2S$ to self-produce.
Question 3: Free Trade Equilibrium
Terms of Trade: $1\text{ Service} = 1\text{ Manufacture}$  ($\frac{P_m}{P_s} = 1$).
Mutually beneficial because $OC_2(M) = \frac{1}{3} < 1 < 2 = OC_1(M)$.
Solving Strategy
Why calculate Country 2 first instead of Country 1?
Country 2 is the smaller capacity economy and completely specializes, which pins down the total volume of world trade!
Country 2 Specialisation
$L_{m,2} = 100, \, L_{s,2} = 0 \implies Y_{m,2} = \mathbf{300 \text{ Manufactures}}$.
Keeps: $150M$ → Exports: $150M$ → Imports: $150S$
Country 2 consumes $(150M, 150S)$ — exactly double its autarky consumption of $75$!
Country 1 Incomplete Specialisation
Country 1 cannot fully specialise: Country 2 can only supply 150 units of manufactures.
Country 1 imports $150M$, exports $150S$, and must self-produce the remaining manufactures domestically.
Solving Country 1's Labour
$\underbrace{4 L_{m,1} + 150}_{\text{Total } M \text{ Consumed}} = \underbrace{8(81.25 - L_{m,1})}_{\text{Total } S \text{ Consumed}}$
Exporting $150S$ takes $\frac{150}{8} = 18.75$ workers $\implies 81.25$ workers remain.
$12 L_{m,1} = 500 \implies L_{m,1} = \mathbf{41\frac{2}{3}}, \quad L_{s,1} = 18.75 + 39.58 = \mathbf{58\frac{1}{3}}$.
Country 1 Trade Outcome
Produces $166\frac{2}{3} M$
+ Imports $150 M$
→
Consumes $\mathbf{316\frac{2}{3} M}$
Produces $466\frac{2}{3} S$
− Exports $150 S$
→
Consumes $\mathbf{316\frac{2}{3} S}$
Autarky vs. Free Trade Summary
Variable Country 1 (Autarky) Country 1 (Trade) Country 2 (Autarky) Country 2 (Trade)
Labour $L_m$ $66\frac{2}{3}$ $41\frac{2}{3}$ (−25) $25$ $100$ (+75)
Labour $L_s$ $33\frac{1}{3}$ $58\frac{1}{3}$ (+25) $75$ $0$ (−75)
Production $Y_m$ $266\frac{2}{3}$ $166\frac{2}{3}$ (−100) $75$ $300$ (+225)
Production $Y_s$ $266\frac{2}{3}$ $466\frac{2}{3}$ (+200) $75$ $0$ (−75)
Consumption ($M=S$) $266\frac{2}{3}$ $316\frac{2}{3}$ (+50) $75$ $150$ (+75)
Gains from Trade Mechanism
Specialisation by CA → World Output Expands → Higher Consumption in Both Nations
Country 1 gains $+50$ units of both goods; Country 2 gains $+75$ units of both goods ($+100\%$ increase!).
Challenge Presenters
“What is the exact range of terms of trade $\frac{P_m}{P_s}$ under which BOTH countries gain from trade?”
Expected Insight
$\frac{1}{3} < \frac{P_m}{P_s} < 2$
If $P_m/P_s < 1/3$, Country 2 refuses to trade (cheaper to self-produce services).
If $P_m/P_s > 2$, Country 1 refuses to trade (cheaper to self-produce manufactures).
Key Exam Takeaways

1. Comparative Advantage Rule

Comparative advantage determines trade patterns, not absolute advantage.

2. PPF Slope $= A_s / A_m$

The opportunity cost of the horizontal good is the magnitude of the PPF slope.

3. Capacity & Specialisation

Smaller trading partners fully specialise; larger economies often partially specialise.

4. Unambiguous Gains

Trade expands consumption possibilities strictly outside the domestic PPF.
